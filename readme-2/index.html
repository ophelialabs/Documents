Learning Library

Organizes learning resources by subject and connects local study guides with university courses and research. The learning app should mirror this directory structure: start with a department or field, follow its learning path, and use school catalogs and research links for deeper study.

Learning domains¶

Humanities and Social Sciences¶

  • Humanities and Social Sciences: economics, consumer behavior, institutions and policy, media and communications, and behavioral and social systems

  • Economics: economic reasoning, markets, incentives, and quantitative methods

  • Consumer Behavior: choices, attention, trust, and decision-making

Physical, Computational, and Applied Sciences¶

  • Computer Science and Computing: development and data-science learning materials

  • Development: software development, systems, and technical practice

  • Data Science: data analysis and computational learning resources

  • Chemistry: chemistry references, learning resources, and computational chemistry

  • Engineering: cross-disciplinary engineering map and specialist domains

  • Mathematics: mathematics learning references

  • Physics: physics resources

The Engineering hub links to Electrical, Materials, Mechanical, Medical, Network, Nuclear, Quantum, and Robotics Engineering. Quantum Engineering includes an integration roadmap and specialized quantum networking and medical systems guides.

Learning platforms and course discovery¶

  • MIT OpenCourseWare: open course materials across science, engineering, mathematics, and humanities

  • Moodle: learning management platform; use each institution’s Moodle site for enrolled courses

  • Percipio: online learning platform

  • NC State course catalog

  • Grainger College of Engineering, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

  • University of California, Berkeley

  • University of California, Santa Barbara

  • University of Tennessee at Chattanooga

  • Top 100 Universities Worldwide (TrekOnward): ranking reference; verify the publisher, methodology, and edition before relying on rankings

Course offerings and catalogs change. Use the current official catalog for prerequisites, degree requirements, and term availability.

Unless a section explicitly cites a ranked table, its schools are a curated global shortlist, not an ordinal ranking; program strengths, degree levels, and research specialties differ. Follow each institution’s official site for current courses and research groups.

Using this platform¶

  1. Choose a department or learning domain that matches the question.

  2. Follow its local guides for concepts, prerequisites, and related disciplines.

  3. Use current school catalogs for official course details and degree requirements.

  4. Use research societies and journals to locate current scholarship.

  5. Compare rankings as one input alongside curriculum, faculty, facilities, cost, access, and research fit.

Ranking and source notes¶

Rankings vary by publisher, year, country, and degree level. The lists above are not presented as a single comparable league table: the 2026 U.S. News nuclear engineering list is explicitly ranked, while other fields are curated global shortlists linked to relevant subject-ranking pages. Check each publisher’s current methodology and edition before using rankings for decisions.

Network reference note¶

TCP port 443 is conventionally used for HTTPS web traffic. This is a general networking reference, not a statement that Iseek itself is configured to use a particular port; deployment details belong in the learning application’s technical configuration.
