# Misinformation and Information Integrity

Misinformation and information integrity study how false, misleading, or manipulated information spreads through media systems and how societies respond.

## Why it matters

Information quality is essential to democratic institutions, trust, and decision-making. When misinformation spreads quickly, it can distort markets, politics, and public reasoning.

## Key mechanisms

### Viral spread

Social and networked media accelerate the spread of information, including falsehoods. Engagement incentives can amplify attention regardless of accuracy.

### Trust collapse

When people cannot distinguish credible from manipulative information, trust in institutions, experts, and media weakens.

### Manipulation and incentive distortion

Bad actors exploit platforms, social identity, and emotional triggers to produce political, financial, or social manipulation.

## Study questions

- What factors make misinformation persuasive?
- How do information ecosystems reward attention over accuracy?
- What behaviors can improve media literacy and trust?
- What institutional responses are effective?

## Response strategies

- media literacy education
- institutional transparency
- provenance tracking
- platform-level trust and safety systems
- public verification systems

## Summary

This topic connects media, policy, psychology, and technology. It shows how information quality is not just a technical problem, but also a social and institutional one.
