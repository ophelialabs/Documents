# Circuits

Circuit theory is the foundation of electrical engineering. It studies how current, voltage, and power behave in interconnected networks of components such as resistors, capacitors, inductors, sources, and switches.

## Core concepts

### Circuit laws

- Ohm's law
- Kirchhoff's current law
- Kirchhoff's voltage law

These laws describe how currents and voltages distribute through a network.

### Network analysis

Circuit analysis methods include:

- nodal analysis
- mesh analysis
- superposition
- Thevenin and Norton equivalents
- transient and steady-state analysis

### Passive elements

- resistors: dissipate energy
- capacitors: store electric field energy
- inductors: store magnetic field energy

These components shape frequency response and time-domain behavior.

### AC and DC behavior

Circuit analysis commonly distinguishes between:

- direct current (DC) systems
- alternating current (AC) systems
- sinusoidal steady-state analysis
- impedance and admittance

## Why circuits matter

Circuit theory is used wherever electrical signals are processed or power is distributed. It is the basis for:

- power systems
- analog electronics
- filters and signal conditioning
- digital logic interfaces
- instrumentation and sensors

## Learning path

### Beginner

- voltage, current, resistance
- series and parallel networks
- basic laws and simplification

### Intermediate

- AC analysis
- transient response
- impedance and phase
- network theorems

### Advanced

- nonlinear circuits
- semiconductor switching behavior
- frequency-selective networks
- power electronics interfaces

## Summary

Circuit analysis provides the language for understanding how electrical systems behave. It is the starting point for nearly every subfield in electrical engineering.
