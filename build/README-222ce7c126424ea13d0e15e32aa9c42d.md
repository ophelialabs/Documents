# Institutions and Policy

Institutions and policy explain how societies organize incentives, authority, and coordination. This section focuses on the rules, structures, and decision systems that shape economic and social life.

## What institutions do

Institutions are the formal and informal systems through which groups make decisions and enforce expectations. They include:

- governments and agencies
- legal frameworks
- market rules
- norms and cultural expectations
- organizations and governance bodies

They matter because they shape behavior even when individuals are rational within their own context.

## Core concepts

### Public choice

Public choice examines how decisions are made in political systems and how incentives influence policy outcomes. It studies the behavior of voters, bureaucrats, and elected officials.

### Regulation and governance

This domain explores how rules are designed to protect welfare, prevent abuse, and manage externalities such as pollution, market power, or information harms.

### Distribution and inequality

Institutions determine who gains from economic activity and how benefits are distributed. This includes taxation, welfare, access to services, and social mobility.

### Institutional design

The quality of institutions affects resilience, trust, and collective action. Strong institutions support:

- accountability
- transparency
- predictable rulemaking
- legitimate coordination

## Why this matters

Institutional design sits at the center of many modern problems, including:

- market power and concentration
- digital platform governance
- consumer protection
- public trust and misinformation
- climate policy and social coordination
