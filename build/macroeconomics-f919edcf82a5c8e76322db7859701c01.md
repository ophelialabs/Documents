# Macroeconomics

Macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole. It focuses on aggregate outcomes such as output, employment, inflation, growth, and the role of policy and institutions in shaping economic performance.

## Core questions

- Why do economies grow or stall?
- What causes inflation or recessions?
- How do governments and central banks shape economic conditions?
- What determines employment, wages, and productivity?
- How do financial systems affect the broader economy?

## Foundations

### Aggregate output

Macroeconomics examines national income, gross domestic product, productivity, and the drivers of long-run growth.

### Business cycles

Economies fluctuate over time. Recessions and booms reflect changes in spending, confidence, investment, and external shocks.

### Inflation and monetary policy

Inflation occurs when prices rise broadly across the economy. Central banks use interest rates and balance-sheet tools to manage inflation and stabilize employment.

### Labor markets

Employment, unemployment, wage dynamics, and labor-force participation are central macroeconomic concerns.

### Fiscal policy

Governments use taxes, spending, and public investment to influence demand, outcomes, and social welfare.

### Financial systems

Banks, credit markets, and financial institutions influence the transmission of shocks across the economy.

## Key concepts

## Why it matters

Macroeconomics helps explain the conditions under which societies can invest, innovate, and provide stability. It is essential for understanding policy choices in times of crisis or rapid change.

## Summary

Macroeconomics provides the big-picture lens for understanding national economies and the policies that shape their performance. It complements microeconomics by focusing on aggregate outcomes rather than individual choice alone.
