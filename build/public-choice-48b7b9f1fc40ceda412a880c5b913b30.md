# Public Choice

Public choice applies economic reasoning to political behavior. It explores how individuals, voters, politicians, and bureaucrats make decisions within political institutions.

## Core ideas

### Incentives in politics

Political actors respond to incentives just as consumers and firms do. Their choices are shaped by elections, budgets, reputation, and power.

### Collective action

Groups often face problems when individual incentives do not align with the public good. This creates coordination challenges and policy trade-offs.

### Interest groups

Special-interest organizations can influence policy in ways that favor narrow groups over broader welfare.

### Voting and representation

Public choice studies how voting rules, representation, and information gaps influence outcomes.

## Why it matters

This perspective explains why institutions sometimes underperform, why policies may favor organized interests, and why reform is often difficult.

## Key topics

- voting behavior
- institutional incentive structures
- bureaucratic behavior
- rent-seeking
- regulation and reform

## Summary

Public choice is a bridge between economics and governance. It reveals how political systems process incentives, conflict, and collective action.
