# Quantitative Economics

Quantitative economics uses mathematical and computational methods to analyze economic behavior, policies, and systems. It is the bridge between theoretical economics and empirical or computational reasoning.

## Why it matters

Economic reasoning becomes more precise when it is formalized with models, optimization, simulation, and data. Quantitative methods help explain patterns in consumption, production, policy design, and market dynamics.

## Core methods

### Mathematical modeling

Economists use equations and formal structures to represent constraints, incentives, and relationships.

### Optimization

Agents and firms often optimize behavior under constraints. This includes utility maximization, profit optimization, and policy trade-offs.

### Dynamic systems

Many economic phenomena evolve over time. Quantitative economics studies dynamics in:

- investment
- capital accumulation
- prices and inflation
- expectations and adjustment

### Simulation and computation

Computational tools are increasingly central in economics, especially when models are too complex for closed-form solutions.

## Key concepts

- equilibrium
- optimization
- dynamic programming
- stochastic systems
- time series
- simulation
- estimation
- comparative statics

## Applications

Quantitative economics is used in:

- forecasting
- policy evaluation
- market design
- labor and public finance analysis
- decision support under uncertainty
- digital economy analysis

## Recommended learning path

### Foundations

- algebra and calculus
- probability and statistics
- optimization basics

### Intermediate

- dynamic models
- time series analysis
- computational simulation

### Advanced

- stochastic modeling
- structural estimation
- macro-finance models
- optimization under uncertainty
