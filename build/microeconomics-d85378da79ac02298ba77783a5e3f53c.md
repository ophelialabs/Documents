# Microeconomics

Microeconomics studies the behavior of individual agents: households, firms, workers, and consumers. It explains how choices are made under scarcity, how prices coordinate activity, and how markets allocate resources.

## Core questions

- How do people decide what to buy and how much?
- How do firms choose output, pricing, and hiring?
- Why do markets sometimes fail or become distorted?
- What role do competition and incentives play in resource allocation?

## Foundations

### Scarcity and choice

All economic behavior begins with scarcity: limited resources, time, and attention. Individuals and organizations must choose among alternatives, which creates trade-offs.

### Utility and demand

Consumers seek to maximize satisfaction or utility subject to budgets and preferences. This produces demand curves, price sensitivity, and substitution effects.

### Production and cost

Firms transform inputs into outputs using labor, capital, and technology. Cost structures determine pricing and production decisions.

### Market equilibrium

Markets move toward equilibrium when supply and demand are balanced. Prices signal scarcity and coordinate behavior across agents.

### Competition and market structure

Different market structures yield different outcomes:

- perfect competition
- monopolistic competition
- oligopoly
- monopoly

These structures affect prices, innovation, and consumer welfare.
