# Media Literacy

Media literacy is the ability to access, analyze, evaluate, and create media in a way that recognizes context, bias, and intent. It is a foundational skill in a media-saturated society and a necessary complement to technical and economic knowledge.

## Why media literacy matters

People encounter media constantly through news, social feeds, advertisements, entertainment, and algorithmic recommendations. Without literacy, it becomes difficult to distinguish credible information from persuasion, manipulation, or entertainment-driven distortion.

## Core skills

### Access

Being able to find and engage with different kinds of media sources responsibly and critically.

### Analysis

Evaluating how messages are constructed, what claims are being made, and what evidence is offered.

### Evaluation

Assessing credibility, context, incentives, and quality of reasoning.

### Creation

Producing media responsibly, with awareness of audience, intent, and ethical effects.

## Key questions

- Who produced this message and why?
- What evidence supports it?
- What assumptions, framing, or omissions are present?
- How might this message affect beliefs or behavior?
- What are the incentives behind the platform or institution sharing it?

## Applications

- political awareness
- public trust
- consumer decision-making
- misinformation response
- digital citizenship

## Summary

Media literacy enables people to interpret media environments critically rather than passively. It is essential to societal resilience, democratic participation, and informed decision-making.
