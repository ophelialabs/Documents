# Political Economy

Political economy studies how power, incentives, institutions, and markets interact. It connects economic reasoning to governance, politics, and the distribution of resources and influence.

## Core questions

- Who gains from policy and institutions?
- How do political systems shape markets?
- Why do certain groups influence policy more than others?
- How do states, firms, and citizens negotiate trade-offs?

## Foundations

### Power and distribution

Political economy examines how resources and influence are distributed across society, and how this affects outcomes in labor, taxes, regulation, and public goods.

### Institutions

Laws, courts, governments, bureaucracies, and informal norms shape economic behavior. Institutions can enable cooperation or reinforce inequality.

### Public choice

Public choice studies how political actors make decisions under incentives, constraints, and imperfect information. It considers voters, politicians, bureaucrats, and interest groups.

### Regulation and rent-seeking

Political economy explains how market power, lobbying, and special-interest influence can distort policy. This includes rent-seeking, regulatory capture, and uneven enforcement.

## Key themes

- state capacity
- redistribution
- public policy design
- market power
- political incentives
- inequality
- governance and accountability

## Why it matters

Political economy is essential for understanding how economic systems are structured by power rather than by pure market logic alone. It helps explain why some societies build strong institutions and others do not.

## Summary

Political economy reveals that markets do not operate in a vacuum. They are embedded in institutions, conflicts, and incentives shaped by power and governance.
