# Consumer Behavior

Consumer behavior examines why individuals choose, reject, prefer, or repeat products, services, ideas, and institutions. It sits at the intersection of economics, psychology, marketing, media, and decision science.

## Why it matters

Consumer behavior is the mechanism through which markets respond to desire, trust, scarcity, and information. It helps explain:

- what drives demand
- why people adopt or reject products
- how prices and signals shape decisions
- how identity, social context, and platform design affect choice
- why attention and trust are strategic assets in digital markets

## Core concepts

### Preferences and utility

Consumers do not choose randomly. They evaluate alternatives based on perceived benefit, satisfaction, and trade-offs. Preferences are shaped by:

- price sensitivity
- perceived quality
- convenience and accessibility
- social identity and status
- trust and reputation

### Information and uncertainty

Consumers often act with incomplete information. This creates uncertainty, risk, and strategic behavior by sellers, influencers, and institutions.

### Framing and attention

The same choice can be perceived differently depending on how it is framed. Attention is scarce, so salience, defaults, social proof, and urgency strongly influence decisions.

### Social identity and norms

Consumers are not isolated actors. Group norms, peer behavior, and social status often guide choices more than simple utility alone.

### Behavioral bias

Real consumer decisions often reflect systematic biases such as:

- loss aversion
- present bias
- confirmation bias
- anchoring
- status-seeking

## Consumer behavior in digital systems

The modern digital environment amplifies the importance of consumer behavior. Platforms shape decisions through:

- personalized recommendation engines
- social proof and reviews
- scarcity tactics and urgency cues
- algorithmic ranking and feed design
- synthetic content and AI-generated persuasion

This means consumer behavior is not only a subject of economics; it is a core lens for understanding digital markets, media systems, and platform power.
