# Behavioral and Social Systems

This section focuses on how individuals and groups make decisions, form identities, and coordinate within broader social systems. It connects psychology, sociology, behavior, and institutional dynamics to the broader humanities and social-science framework.

## Core concerns

Behavioral and social systems investigate:

- how individuals make decisions under uncertainty
- how norms and identity shape action
- how groups coordinate and conflict
- how social trust affects cooperation
- how digital environments alter human behavior

## Key concepts

### Decision theory

Decision theory studies how people evaluate risk, uncertainty, and alternatives. It explains why choices are not always rational in a narrow economic sense, but remain understandable within a broader social and cognitive context.

### Social trust

Trust is a foundational element of cooperation. It supports markets, communities, institutions, and broader social coordination.

### Identity and norms

People do not act solely as isolated consumers or workers. Identity, social status, group membership, and shared values influence behavior.

### Digital behavior

Online platforms reshape attention, social comparison, group identity, and persuasion. This creates new forms of engagement, trust, and manipulation.

## Why this section matters

This domain connects economic theory to lived experience. It helps explain why markets and institutions are not only technical systems but also social systems shaped by human perception, trust, and interaction.
