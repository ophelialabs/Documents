# Behavioral Economics

Behavioral economics studies how real people make decisions in ways that deviate from perfectly rational utility maximization. It combines psychology with economics to explain observed human behavior.

## Core concepts

### Bounded rationality

People do not have unlimited time, information, or cognitive capacity. Decisions are often based on heuristics rather than exhaustive optimization.

### Biases and heuristics

Common decision patterns include:

- loss aversion
- present bias
- status quo bias
- anchoring
- confirmation bias

### Behavioral design

Organizations use behavioral insights to improve choices in healthcare, education, savings, and public policy.

## Applications

- nudging
- consumer policy
- health behavior
- financial decision-making
- digital engagement design

## Why it matters

Behavioral economics is essential for understanding why markets, institutions, and digital systems do not always produce the outcomes predicted by classical models.

## Summary

Behavioral economics explains the gap between idealized rational choice and real human behavior. It is crucial for understanding individuals, institutions, and policy in practice.
