# Signals and Systems

Signals and systems studies how information and physical quantities vary over time and how systems transform them. It provides the mathematical framework for understanding communication, control, filtering, and instrumentation.

## Core ideas

### Signals

Signals may be:

- continuous-time or discrete-time
- analog or digital
- periodic or aperiodic
- deterministic or random

Examples include voltage waveforms, audio recordings, sensor outputs, and communication streams.

### Systems

A system takes an input signal and produces an output signal. Systems may be linear, time-invariant, causal, stable, and memoryless.

### Transform methods

Key analytical tools include:

- Fourier series
- Fourier transform
- Laplace transform
- Z-transform

These tools help describe frequency content and system response.

### Filtering and response

Signals and systems is essential for:

- noise reduction
- resonance and bandwidth analysis
- control and feedback
- modulation and detection
- sensor signal conditioning

## Important concepts

- convolution
- frequency response
- transfer functions
- impulse response
- stability
- sampling and reconstruction

## Why it matters

This field connects physics and mathematics to real systems. It allows engineers to reason about signal quality, system dynamics, and how information flows through engineered processes.

## Summary

Signals and systems is the mathematical backbone of electrical engineering. It explains how signals are generated, processed, and recovered across communication, control, and measurement systems.
