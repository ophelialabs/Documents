# Social Trust and Cooperation

Social trust is the willingness to rely on others, institutions, and shared norms in uncertain situations. It is a foundational condition for cooperation, markets, governance, and public life.

## Why it matters

Trust reduces transaction costs, supports collective action, and enables long-term cooperation. In democratic and economic systems, trust is a key ingredient in legitimacy and resilience.

## Related concepts

- social capital
- cooperation
- legitimacy
- institutional confidence
- civic engagement

## Drivers of trust

Trust depends on several factors:

- reliability and competence
- transparency and honesty
- fairness and reciprocity
- consistent institutional behavior
- social identity and group belonging

## Risks to trust

- misinformation
- corruption
- manipulation
- inequality
- institutional failure
- platform-driven distortion

## Applications

- community building
- public policy design
- organizational culture
- digital ecosystems
- civic participation

## Summary

Trust is not just an emotional attribute; it is a structural resource that enables social and economic coordination. Understanding it is essential to analyzing institutions, market systems, and digital society.
