# Media Theory

Media theory studies how communication systems shape culture, knowledge, perception, and power. It asks not only what is communicated, but how media forms affect society and how audiences interpret messages.

## Why media theory matters

Media are not neutral channels. They structure attention, frame meaning, and influence how people understand reality. Media theory helps explain:

- how narrative forms create social meaning
- how institutions control public communication
- how technology changes the scale and speed of attention
- how digital systems reshape participation and persuasion

## Core ideas

### Medium as message

Marshall McLuhan’s idea that the form of a medium shapes the content and social effects of communication remains foundational. The medium influences how information is perceived, not just what is said.

### Attention and framing

Media systems determine what becomes salient, what is ignored, and how issues are interpreted. Framing shapes public understanding and political discourse.

### Mass and networked media

Traditional mass media and modern networked media differ in distribution, participation, speed, and feedback. Networked media enable participatory culture while also expanding misinformation and manipulation.

### Power and representation

Media institutions are embedded in political and economic power. They influence public narratives and often reproduce dominant social structures.

## Key questions

- How does the form of a medium shape meaning?
- Who controls message distribution?
- How does media change public attention and action?
- What are the social consequences of personalization and algorithmic ranking?
