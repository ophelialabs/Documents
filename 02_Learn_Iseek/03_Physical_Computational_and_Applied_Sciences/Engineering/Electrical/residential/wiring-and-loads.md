# Residential Wiring and Load Design

This page introduces the design logic behind residential electrical systems: how power enters a home, how it is distributed, and how load requirements are calculated for safety and performance.

## Core concepts

### Service entry

The service entrance brings utility power into the dwelling and includes:

- service drop or lateral
- meter
- disconnect
- panelboard or load center

### Branch circuits

Branch circuits distribute power to fixed loads and receptacles. Typical categories include:

- lighting circuits
- small appliance circuits
- dedicated appliance circuits
- outdoor and garage circuits

### Load calculation

A residential design must account for:

- connected load
- demand load
- diversity and simultaneity
- future expansion and code minimums

## Design thinking

When sizing a system, engineers consider:

- available service capacity
- conductor size and voltage drop
- breaker ratings
- safety margins
- code compliance

## Summary

Residential wiring and load design is about balancing safety, capacity, and usability. Good design ensures that circuits are not overloaded while remaining practical for everyday home use.
